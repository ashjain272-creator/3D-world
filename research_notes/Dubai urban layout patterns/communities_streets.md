# Dubai Communities & Districts: Internal Layout, Street Networks, Plot and Block Sizes

Method note: research used web search snippets plus listing/developer pages. Page fetches to en.wikipedia.org, wikiwand.com and researchgate.net failed (hosts not allowed in this session), so Wikipedia-derived figures come from search-result summaries, not full pages. Plot sizes for villa communities mostly come from broker listings (Property Finder, Bayut, Dubizzle). Treat them as indicative ranges, not official plot schedules. Unit conversion: 1 sq ft = 0.0929 m².

## Villa communities (Emirates Hills, Arabian Ranches, Springs/Meadows, Dubai Hills Estate, Jumeirah Park, Palm fronds): hierarchy, plots, setbacks, amenities

### Takeaway
Dubai's master-planned villa communities form a clear plot-size ladder. Springs townhouses are about 250-400 m². Jumeirah Park and Meadows villas are about 550-1,100 m². Palm frond villas are about 1,200-1,600 m², and Emirates Hills mansions about 1,100-5,400 m². Each community is a gated cluster of sub-communities wrapped around a central amenity such as a golf course, lake chain or big park. Official street-hierarchy and setback numbers were mostly not found. The structural pattern (gated sub-communities, loops and cul-de-sacs around water and golf) is well attested.

**Setbacks / coverage (regulatory)**

- The 2025 "Home First" Dubai Building Code update cut the minimum villa setback to 1.5 m and added a further 1.5 m setback for rooftop floors (applies to Emirati family housing). — [The National](https://thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2025/03/17/dubai-emirati-housing-plan); [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/dubai-building-code-update)
- Low-confidence training material gives a maximum of 70% plot coverage including all structures and a minimum 2.0 m street setback. — [FlipHTML5 course module](https://fliphtml5.com/amnxb/stsi)
- A broker guide says setbacks vary by master plan, with larger setbacks in villa zones such as Meydan and Dubai Hills Estate. — [FAM Properties blog](https://famproperties.com/blog/avoid-costly-mistakes)

### Inferences
- **Plot-size ladder for a generator (areas from listings; frontages assume plot depth of 1.3-2.5x width):**
  - Townhouse / Springs: 250-400 m², about 10-14 m frontage.
  - Mid villa (Meadows, Jumeirah Park, Ranches): 550-1,100 m², about 20-30 m frontage.
  - Palm frond: 1,200-1,600 m², about 20-30 m beach frontage on a long, deep lot.
  - Mansion (Emirates Hills, DHE mansions): 2,300-4,200 m², about 40-60 m frontage.
- **Palm frond geometry:** a frond is a single spine road with one row of villas on each side, each backing onto the beach. Tip plots are much larger. This follows from the "200 m beachfront" and "tip plot" listings. It is inferred, not documented.
- **Topology:** the repeated pattern is a master community, then gated sub-communities (roughly 15-30 per large master plan), then curving loop roads and cul-de-sacs. Premium rows face the golf fairway, lake or park ("backs onto", "full golf view", "cul-de-sac" are common listing terms).
- **Open space:** golf takes about 11-15% of the area (DHE: 120 ha of 1,093 ha; Ranches about 100 ha of 668 ha). Total open space in DHE is about 13%.
- **Coverage:** use a front setback of about 3-6 m and side setbacks of 1.5-3 m, with 40-70% plot coverage. The minimums (1.5 m, 70%) come from regulation and training material. Larger master-plan setbacks are typical but were not quantified.

### Gaps
- No official numbers for loop-road and collector widths, cul-de-sac lengths or gate counts per community. Developer master-plan PDFs could not be fetched.
- Community-centre placement (retail, mosque, school) was not documented quantitatively. Anecdotally each master community has a central "town centre" or souk near the main gate, e.g. Arabian Ranches Souk and DHE Mall, but this was not verified in this session.
- No plot sizes were found for Arabian Ranches villas. Jumeirah Islands cluster and lake layout was not found.

## Older villa areas (Jumeirah 1-3, Al Barsha, Umm Suqeim): grid vs organic, plot sizes, walls

### Takeaway
The older non-freehold villa districts use a rectilinear municipal grid. Plots are mostly about 10,000-15,000 sq ft (930-1,400 m²) and often carry permission for 1-3 villas as a "villa compound" with boundary walls. These areas are less regular than the newer master-plan curvilinear layouts.

### Cited Findings
- Umm Suqeim 1 and 2 plots are listed at 10,000 sq ft (about 929 m²). They are GCC-national-only and non-freehold. — [Property Finder Umm Suqeim 2](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/en/plp/buy/land-for-sale-dubai-umm-suqeim-umm-suqeim-2-16071779); [Bayut](https://bayut.com/property/details-9750227.html)
- An Umm Suqeim 2 corner plot of 15,074 sq ft (about 1,400 m²) is zoned "Residential – Villa Compound", fronts two roads, and carries permission for 3 villas. — [Property Finder](https://propertyfinder.ae/en/plp/buy/land-for-sale-dubai-umm-suqeim-umm-suqeim-2-12822549.html); [Bayut](https://www.bayut.com/property/details-15983003.html)
- An Al Barsha 3 villa sits on a 15,000 sq ft plot. An Umm Al Sheif plot of 15,000 sq ft has permission for up to 3 villas. — [Bayut](https://www.bayut.com/property/details-9469013.html); [Bayut](https://www.bayut.com/property/details-5356190.html)
- In the traditional Dubai typology the courtyard is central to the house, for social and climate reasons. Modern neighbourhood planning tries to reconcile this with grid-plot planning. — [ResearchGate: "A return to the old landscape?"](https://researchgate.net/publication/326631039_A_return_to_the_old_landscape_Balancing_physical_planning_ideals_and_cultural_constraints_in_Dubai's_residential_neighborhoods) (snippet only)
- An LSE Middle East Centre blog discusses spatial segregation and low-density villa sprawl in Dubai's metropolisation. — [LSE MEC](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/mec/2022/01/11/metropolisation-and-spatial-segregation-in-gulf-cities-the-case-of-dubai/)

### Inferences
- **Generator rule for "old villa" districts:** an orthogonal grid of blocks, each holding 2 rows of back-to-back plots. Plots are about 930-1,400 m² (e.g. 30 x 31 m to 35 x 40 m). Use a perimeter boundary wall about 2-3 m high with 1-3 villas per plot. The local street grid has fairly short blocks, with mosques at block-cluster intervals. The wall height and street spacing are not sourced.
- **Mixing rule:** an occasional double plot or corner compound is about 2x the typical area.

### Gaps
- No sourced figures for block length, street widths or boundary-wall heights in Jumeirah, Al Barsha or Umm Suqeim. The academic paper text could not be fetched.

## High-rise districts (Dubai Marina, JLT, JBR, Business Bay, Downtown, DIFC): blocks, podium-tower typology, water bodies, boulevards

### Takeaway
Each Dubai high-rise district is a superblock organised around one water body or one ring boulevard:
- Marina: a canal about 3-4 km long, at least 70 m wide, in a district of about 287 ha.
- JLT: about 26 clusters (A-Z) of usually 3 towers each, arranged around 3-4 artificial lakes.
- Business Bay: about 240-260 towers on both banks of the Dubai Canal, in about 4.4 km².
- Downtown: about 200 ha, ringed by the 3.5 km Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Boulevard, around the 12 ha Burj Lake.
- JBR: a 1.7 km beachfront strip of about 36-40 towers on a continuous 2-level retail podium ("The Walk").

### Cited Findings
**Dubai Marina**
- HOK master-planned it in 1999 for Emaar as a "canal city". — [Architect Magazine](https://www.architectmagazine.com/?p=187233); [Archnet](https://archnet.org/sites/6694)
- One source gives a 4 km canal with a minimum width of 70 m, providing 11 km of water frontage. Others say about 3 km or 3.5 km. — [World Construction Network](https://www.worldconstructionnetwork.com/projects/dubai-marina); [Gulf Construction](https://gulfconstructiononline.com/Article/4831/World-class_Waterfront)
- The district is about 287 ha. Phase I covered 10 ha with 6 towers and 64 villas. The full plan has about 200 high-rises. — [Architizer](https://architizer.com/projects/dubai-marina-2); [Multiplex](https://www.multiplex.global/projects/dubai-marina-stages-1-2/)

**JBR (Jumeirah Beach Residence)**
- A 1.7 km shoreline development of about 40 towers in clusters. ULI counts 36 residential towers of 39-54 storeys, plus 4 hotel towers. — [Bayut](https://bayut.com/area-guides/jumeirah-beach-residence-jbr); [ULI case study](https://casestudies.uli.org/metro_area/dubai)
- "The Walk" is a 1.7 km strip on the ground and plaza levels of the podium. It has 2 levels and 311 retail outlets. — [ULI](https://casestudies.uli.org/metro_area/dubai); [Wikipedia: The Walk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walk,_Dubai) (via search snippet)
- The floor stack is B+G+M+P+UP+42-44 (basement, ground, mezzanine, podium and upper-podium parking, then a tower of about 42-44 floors). The design is a slab-block ("Plattenbau") typology. — [Dar](https://dar.ae/?p=25990)
- Jumeirah Gate (2018) is 290 m tall with a 3-level podium. Its two towers are linked at the base and the top. — [Archilovers / Killa Design](https://www.archilovers.com/projects/263418/jumeirah-gate-the-address-hotel-residences.html)

**JLT (Jumeirah Lake Towers)**
- 26 clusters lettered A-Z, typically 3 towers each. Cluster I is a mixed-use exception. — [Wikipedia JLT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jumeirah_Lake_Towers) (via search snippet); [Property Finder Cluster J](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/blog/jlt-cluster-j/)
- Tower count is 79-87 depending on source: 79 along 4 lakes, or 80 including an 8-tower embankment. — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/arts/neighbourhood-guide-jumeirah-lakes-towers-dubai-1.150169); [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Jumeirah_Lake_Towers) (low reliability)
- Three current lakes: Lake Almas West, Lake Almas East and JLT Lake. A fourth, Lake Elucio, was drained in 2012. — [Emirates 24/7](https://www.emirates247.com/news/blame-game-drowns-the-hope-of-lakes-for-jlt-2011-10-17-1.423769); Wikipedia via snippet
- CDM Smith describes the infrastructure contract as a 180 ha development with 28 clusters. — [CDM Smith](https://cdmsmith.com/en-ME/Client-Solutions/Projects/Jumeirah-Lakes-Towers-Dubai)

**Business Bay**
- About 46.9 million sq ft (4.36 km²) along the Dubai Water Canal. Development plots line both banks. The original plan had about 240-260 towers of mixed residential, office and hotel use. — [getfuture.ae](https://getfuture.ae/blog/blog/business-bay-dubai-from-visionary-masterplan-to-global-commercial-powerhouse); [Systematica](https://www.systematica.net/project/dubai-business-bay-master-plan/); [kestates](https://kestates.ae/en/dubai-area-guide/business-bay/)
- Business Bay lies directly south of Downtown, between Sheikh Zayed Road and Al Khail Road. — [kestates](https://kestates.ae/en/dubai-area-guide/business-bay/)

**Downtown Dubai**
- 500 acres (about 200 ha). — [Emaar communities](https://communities.emaar.com/en/communities/downtown-dubai/discover/about-downtown-dubai/); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/property/downtown-burj-dubai-1.28472)
- Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Boulevard is 3.5 km long and nearly encircles the district. It was renamed in 2012. — [Burj Khalifa site](https://www.burjkhalifa.ae/en/downtown-dubai); [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/business/worlds-most-visited-boulevard-in-dubai-named-sheikh-mohammed-bin-rashid-boulevard-wtlbd7zx); [Luxhabitat](https://www.luxhabitat.ae/areas/dubai/downtown-dubai/)
- Burj Lake (Dubai Fountain lake) is 12 ha (120,000 m², about 30 acres). — [Emaar](https://www.emaar.com/ru/properties/the-residence-burj-khalifa); [Luxhabitat](https://www.luxhabitat.ae/areas/dubai/downtown-dubai/)

### Inferences
- **Downtown ring:** a 3.5 km ring encloses about 0.97 km² if circular (radius about 560 m). The real loop is irregular and elongated. Burj Khalifa, the Dubai Mall and the 12 ha lake sit at the centre. Towers line the inside and outside of the ring, and SZR forms the north-west edge.
- **JLT:** 26 clusters x 3 towers ≈ 78-80 towers on about 180 ha, i.e. about 7 ha per cluster including the lakes. Cluster layout: a roughly square or triangular plinth (podium/parking) with 3 towers around it. Clusters line the lake edges in a chain that is about 3 lakes long, parallel to SZR, across from the Marina.
- **Marina:** canal width ≥70 m. Tower plots sit on a promenade on both banks, with JBR between the Marina and the beach. That gives a 3-band cross-section from inland: SZR, Marina towers, canal, Marina towers, JBR towers with podium, beach.
- **Podium/tower rule for a generator:** a podium of 3-6 levels (G + mezzanine + 2-4 parking levels, about 15-25 m) carrying 1-3 slender towers of 40-55 floors (about 150-250 m), with outliers above 300 m. Podium height comes from the JBR B+G+M+P+UP stack. Tower spacing was not sourced.

### Gaps
- No sourced tower-to-tower spacing or podium-edge tower setbacks. Dubai Building Code tables could not be fetched.
- DIFC area (often quoted as 110 acres) and Gate Avenue details were not confirmed in this session.
- No sourced block dimensions for Business Bay plots.

## Old town (Deira, Bur Dubai, Al Fahidi/Bastakiya, Al Ras, Shindagha): street width, courtyard houses, souks, wind towers, grain

### Takeaway
The historic core has a very fine grain: plots of about 15 x 15 m, lanes (sikka) of about 2-2.5 m, and alleys taking up about 13% of the land. Courtyard houses are packed tight with wind towers (barjeel). In the 1890s Al Fahidi held about 60 houses, half of which were demolished in the 1980s.

### Cited Findings
- Sikka are narrow alleys of varying width that divide the historic residential areas into blocks. House main and secondary doors open onto them. — [Dubai Culture](https://dubaiculture.gov.ae/en/attractions/heritage-sites/al-fahidi-historical-neighbourhood/); [Google Arts & Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/zAWRZ_j5CQugJw)
- Lanes between houses in Old Dubai are about 7-8 ft wide (about 2.1-2.4 m). This is from a travel source, so treat it as anecdotal. — [Middle East Journeys](https://middleeastjourneys.com/dubai-old-town-architecture/)
- A former Deira neighbourhood had close-knit houses on 50 x 50 ft (about 15 x 15 m) plots. The district had traditional courtyard housing, mid- and low-rise buildings, and narrow alleys taking about 13% of the area. — [ResearchGate: Urban Redevelopment Trauma: The Story of a Dubai Neighbourhood](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/267397270_Urban_Redevelopment_Trauma_The_Story_of_a_Dubai_Neighbourhood) (snippet)
- Al Fahidi courtyard houses were linked to barjeel wind towers for cooling. The style blends Emirati and Persian influences and uses gypsum/coral walls. The area held about 60 houses, dates from the 1890s, and about half was demolished in the 1980s for offices. — [Dubai Culture](https://dubaiculture.ae/en/attractions/heritage-sites/al-fahidi-historical-neighbourhood); [Yahoo/Telegraph](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/surprising-corner-dubai-saved-annihilation-180000146.html); [Propertyfinder blog](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/blog/al-fahidi-historical/)
- Bur Dubai has narrow, winding traditional sikkas, with plots densely packed along the creek and seafront. — [ResearchGate CityWalks morphology](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/376456980_Towards_a_socially_vibrant_city_exploring_urban_typologies_and_morphologies_of_the_emerging_CityWalks_in_Dubai) (snippet)
- Compact, narrow alleys give self-shading and channel the prevailing winds. — [arXiv passive cooling review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.09365)

### Inferences
- **Generator rules for the old town:**
  - Organic, irregular block outlines.
  - Plots of 12-20 m square, holding 1-2 storey courtyard houses with a courtyard of about 25-35% of the plot.
  - Sikka lanes of 1.5-3 m that dead-end or wind. A few wider souk spines of about 4-6 m, roofed with timber or barasti arcades (souk width not sourced).
  - Wind towers of about 1 per house on larger houses, about 3-4 m square and rising about 5-8 m above the roof (tower dimensions not sourced).
  - Alleys take about 13% of land and built cover is about 80-85%.

### Gaps
- No measured souk arcade widths, wind-tower dimensions or Al Ras block sizes. GIS morphology papers were paywalled or unreachable.

## Road widths and lanes: SZR, collectors, local streets; interchange spacing

### Takeaway
Sheikh Zayed Road (E11) runs about 6-8 lanes each way through urban Dubai, with parallel service roads and the Metro Red Line running alongside (elevated in the median or beside it). Interchanges are numbered (1st to 7th+). Official right-of-way (ROW) widths and interchange spacing were not found. Dubai lane widths are about 3.0-3.75 m, with up to about 4.5 m on main arterials (low confidence).

### Cited Findings
- E11/SZR has 7-8 lanes in each direction through much of Dubai. Earlier stretches had 4 lanes per direction. — [E 11 road (Wikipedia mirror)](https://en.wikipedia-on-ipfs.org/wiki/E_11_road_(United_Arab_Emirates)); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/uae%2Finterchanges-to-be-redesigned-on-congested-key-road-1.435697)
- SZR interchanges are numbered, e.g. Interchange 5 near the American University in Dubai and the "7th interchange" project at Al Yalayis. — [RTA project](https://rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/home/news-and-media/AllProjects/ProjectDetails/phase+1+of+7th+interchange+al+yalayes+and+al+asayel+road+projects)
- Sheikh Mohamed Bin Zayed Road (E311) was widened to a 12-lane divided highway, i.e. 6 lanes each way, with inner and outer shoulders. — [CDM Smith](https://cdmsmith.com/en/Client-Solutions/Projects/Sheikh-Mohamed-Bin-Zayed-Road)
- Dubai's ROW is defined as the public road plus the area out to adjacent plot boundaries, per DM-approved plans.
