# Dubai Strings Tower Nodes Along One Spine

Dubai is a **coastal ribbon city**. Five roads run parallel to the Gulf shore: the beach road, Sheikh Zayed Road (SZR, about 2 km inland), Al Khail Road, Mohammed bin Zayed Road and Emirates Road. Each band inland is newer and more suburban than the one before. Strung along the SZR spine are **six discrete tower clusters holding 309 buildings of 100 m or more**, and the gaps between them are filled by low-rise villa belts on both sides. The old town sits at the north-east end of the corridor. It is a fine-grained knot of 2–3 m lanes and courtyard houses on both banks of Dubai Creek, where the souks face each other across the water. Waterways organise the districts. The Creek extends as an engineered canal through Business Bay and then back to the sea. The Marina is a 3–4 km inland canal ringed by towers. The Palms push villas offshore. Amenities follow simple rules. Mosques come in two tiers, a small daily mosque within roughly 500 m of every home and a larger Friday mosque at each community centre. Super-regional malls sit on the spine, wired to metro stations by enclosed bridges. Churches and temples are grouped into one or two walled compounds rather than scattered. All of this converts readily into procedural rules. The catch is that a 600 m game city cannot scale Dubai uniformly: uniform scaling would shrink towers to toothpicks. The generator therefore keeps Dubai's **sequence, adjacency and contrasts** and compresses its distances. The rules at the end of this report use three separate scale factors and record the real value behind every parameter.

A note on evidence. The underlying research ran with Wikipedia, Dubai Municipality and several news sites blocked, so many figures come from search-engine summaries of the cited pages and from property listings. Where a number is an estimate, inference or design choice rather than a sourced fact, the report says so.

## Five coast-parallel bands and one road that cuts the city in two

The organising fact of Dubai's plan is its linearity. SZR runs **parallel to the coast about 2 km inland**. The strip between road and sea is the city's largest continuous urban area. It is mostly low-rise residential and grows denser and more commercial toward the centre ([Planetizen](https://www.planetizen.com/node/41919)). SZR itself runs **more than 70 km**, links most key destinations, and "cuts the city in two" ([Transform Transport](https://transformtransport.org/media/articles/sheikh-zayed-road/)). At the regional scale, Dubai, Sharjah and Ajman form a **conurbation more than 80 km long** along the Gulf ([ResearchGate, "The Boom"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317584226_The_Boom_Population_and_Urban_Growth_of_Dubai_City)). A space-syntax model of about 1,100 km² of metropolitan Dubai confirms the north–south linear pattern. It also shows the cost: the historic Deira core has limited accessibility to the enlarged region it spawned ([Springer, Hovsepian et al.](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-11306-1_12)). Growth has run mostly south-west toward the Palm, and **more than 11 km² of sea** was converted to built land between 1972 and 2011 ([GISRUK 2012](https://www.geos.ed.ac.uk/~gisteac/proceedingsonline/GISRUK2012/Papers/presentation-65.pdf)).

Behind SZR, three more arterials repeat the pattern further inland. Al Khail Road (the Dubai section of E44) passes Sports City, Jumeirah Village Triangle and JVC ([Property Finder](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/blog/highways/)). E311 runs parallel to SZR through urban areas. E611, Emirates Road, acts as an outer bypass for the suburban communities ([Property Finder](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/blog/highways/)). No source gave measured spacings between these roads. A map-based estimate in the south-west half of the city puts Al Khail at roughly 5–7 km from the coast, E311 at 13–16 km and E611 at 20–25 km. Near the old core the bands converge and bend around the Creek. Read from the sea inland, the cross-section runs as follows: beach and hotels; a low villa belt (Jumeirah, Umm Suqeim, Al Safa); SZR, with towers at nodes; a mixed band of mid-rise, light industry and villa estates (Al Barsha, Al Quoz, Emirates Living, Dubai Hills); Al Khail; master-planned suburbs and sports and logistics districts; and finally desert and new satellite centres.

SZR is a barrier as much as a link. It carries **7–8 lanes in each direction** through much of Dubai ([E 11 road, Wikipedia mirror](https://en.wikipedia-on-ipfs.org/wiki/E_11_road_(United_Arab_Emirates))) at numbered interchanges ([RTA](https://rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/home/news-and-media/AllProjects/ProjectDetails/phase+1+of+7th+interchange+al+yalayes+and+al+asayel+road+projects)). E311 was widened to 12 lanes, six each way ([CDM Smith](https://cdmsmith.com/en/Client-Solutions/Projects/Sheikh-Mohamed-Bin-Zayed-Road)). The official right-of-way tables sit in the RTA Geometric Design Manual, which the researchers could not retrieve ([Dubai Legislation Portal](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2025/Administrative%20Resolution%20No.%20(273)%20of%202025%20Regulating%20Works%20in%20the%20Right%20of%20Way.html)). Generic engineering values imply an SZR right-of-way of roughly 100–120 m including service roads. On the same unsourced basis, interchanges fall every 2–5 km. Dubai's 1st to 7th interchanges span about 20 km or more from Trade Centre toward Jebel Ali.

The Metro reinforces the spine. The **Red Line runs about 67 km with 35 stations** parallel to SZR, mostly elevated in or beside the median. The **Green Line is 22.5 km with 20 stations** and loops through Deira and Bur Dubai ([The Metro Rail Guy](https://themetrorailguy.com/metro-rail-systems/dubai-metro-information-stations-route-map-fare-prices-nol-pass-hours-timings/); [Wikipedia: Dubai Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_Metro)). That works out to about 1.9 km between stations on the Red Line and 1.1 km on the Green. Planetizen describes Red Line stations as about a mile apart at activity nodes such as malls and business centres ([Planetizen](https://www.planetizen.com/node/41919)). An 11-stop tram serves Al Sufouh, the Marina and JBR ([UrbanRail.net](https://urbanrail.net/as/dub/dubai.htm)). A 30 km Blue Line under construction will take rail to **131 km and 78 stations** ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/uae%2Ftransport%2Fdubai-metro-at-16-from-visionary-launch-to-future-with-blue-line-1.500261259)).

## Six tower nodes, not a continuous wall

Dubai looks like one long skyline on postcards, but its height is concentrated in nodes. The Council on Tall Buildings counts **six clusters with 309 buildings of 100 m or more**, which makes Dubai a "tall, polycentric city". **Downtown plus Business Bay holds 88, Dubai Marina 87, Jumeirah Lakes Towers 61 and the SZR/DIFC strip 55**. CTBUH defines a cluster as at least five buildings of 100 m or more. It names SZR as the element that unifies the city and Deira as one of the earliest tall clusters ([CTBUH, Safarik](https://global.ctbuh.org/resources/papers/3816-Safarik_PolycentricCities.pdf)). The two super-nodes, Downtown and the Marina, lie about **16 km apart in a straight line** and about 22 km by road ([geodatos](https://www.geodatos.net/en/distances/cities/united-arab-emirates/dubai-rgn); [apil properties](https://apilproperties.com/question/communities/how-far-is-the-addresss-dubai-marina-from-downtown-dubai)). Only the SZR Trade Centre strip behaves like a true wall. It is almost entirely high-rise from Trade Centre Roundabout to the 2nd Interchange, a stretch of about 3 km. Emirates Towers on that strip rises to 333 m ([Wikipedia Trade Centre 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_Centre_1); [Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/points-of-interest/emirates-towers/1322222)).

Using map-based estimates rather than cited figures, the order along SZR from north-east to south-west is as follows. The Deira and Bur Dubai old core comes first at km 0, then the Trade Centre wall and DIFC at km 4–7, then Downtown and Business Bay on the inland side at km 7–10. A long mid-rise gap follows through Al Wasl, Al Quoz and Al Barsha, with a small node at Mall of the Emirates. Media City and Tecom sit at km 20–23. At km 23–27 the Marina and JBR on the sea side face JLT on the inland side, so one node straddles the spine. Beyond km 30 come the low industrial port and free zone at Jebel Ali. Dubai has **73 buildings of 200 m or more** ([Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Dubai)). Burj Khalifa (828 m) gives Downtown a single dominant peak. Height drops steeply rather than gradually. Emirates Hills, the Springs and the Meadows sit directly inland of JLT, so 40–80 storey towers give way to 1–2 storey villas within roughly 0.5–1 km. This is an inference from the documented adjacency ([apil properties](https://www.apilproperties.com/uae/dubai/communities/emirates-hills)).

### Each tower district is a superblock wrapped around one water body or one ring road

Within a node, the high-rise districts repeat a small set of layouts. **Dubai Marina** was master-planned by HOK in 1999 as a "canal city" ([Architect Magazine](https://www.architectmagazine.com/?p=187233)). Its canal is about 3–4 km long and at least 70 m wide, with about 11 km of water frontage ([World Construction Network](https://www.worldconstructionnetwork.com/projects/dubai-marina)). The district covers about 287 ha and holds about 200 high-rises ([Architizer](https://architizer.com/projects/dubai-marina-2)). Seen from inland, the cross-section is SZR, Marina towers, canal, Marina towers, JBR, beach. **JBR** is a 1.7 km beachfront run of 36 residential towers of 39–54 storeys plus 4 hotel towers. They stand on a continuous podium whose two lower levels form "The Walk", with 311 retail outlets ([ULI](https://casestudies.uli.org/metro_area/dubai)). The JBR floor stack is the clearest source for podium height. It runs basement, ground, mezzanine, podium parking and upper-podium parking, then a tower of 42–44 floors ([Dar](https://dar.ae/?p=25990)).

**JLT** consists of **26 clusters lettered A to Z, typically 3 towers each**, around three remaining artificial lakes ([Property Finder Cluster J](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/blog/jlt-cluster-j/); [Emirates 24/7](https://www.emirates247.com/news/blame-game-drowns-the-hope-of-lakes-for-jlt-2011-10-17-1.423769)). It sits in a development of about 180 ha ([CDM Smith](https://cdmsmith.com/en-ME/Client-Solutions/Projects/Jumeirah-Lakes-Towers-Dubai)), which implies roughly 7 ha per cluster including water. **Business Bay** covers about 4.4 km² of plots on both banks of the Dubai Canal. It was planned for 240–260 towers and lies directly south of Downtown, between SZR and Al Khail ([Systematica](https://www.systematica.net/project/dubai-business-bay-master-plan/); [kestates](https://kestates.ae/en/dubai-area-guide/business-bay/)). **Downtown** is about 200 ha ([Emaar](https://communities.emaar.com/en/communities/downtown-dubai/discover/about-downtown-dubai/)). The 3.5 km Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Boulevard nearly encircles it ([Burj Khalifa](https://www.burjkhalifa.ae/en/downtown-dubai)), and the 12 ha Burj Lake sits at its centre ([Luxhabitat](https://www.luxhabitat.ae/areas/dubai/downtown-dubai/)). The Dubai Mall, at about 350,000 m² of leasable area, sits beside the lake. An 820 m enclosed link carrying 13,500 people per hour connects it to the Red Line ([Emaar](https://burjkhalifa.ae/en/downtown-dubai/dubai-mall); [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/dubai-mall-metro-link-opens)).

The common building block is the **podium-and-tower**. A podium of 3–6 levels (ground, mezzanine and 2–4 parking decks, about 15–25 m) carries one to three slender towers. In practice the podium is often continuous along a frontage, as at JBR, or shared by a twin pair linked at base and crown, as at the 290 m Jumeirah Gate with its 3-level podium ([Archilovers](https://www.archilovers.com/projects/263418/jumeirah-gate-the-address-hotel-residences.html)). Typical towers run 40–55 floors, or about 150–250 m. Tower-to-tower spacing and podium-edge setbacks were not found in any accessible source, so the generator values for those are design choices.

## Grain coarsens roughly tenfold from the Creek to the superblock

The old town is a different city in miniature. **Al Fahidi (Bastakiya)**, on the Bur Dubai bank, dates from the 1890s and once held **about 60 houses separated by narrow winding lanes** ([Wikipedia Al Bastakiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Bastakiya)). About half were demolished in the 1980s for offices ([Dubai Culture](https://dubaiculture.ae/en/attractions/heritage-sites/al-fahidi-historical-neighbourhood)). The lanes, called sikkas, divide the residential areas into blocks, and house doors open onto them ([Dubai Culture](https://dubaiculture.gov.ae/en/attractions/heritage-sites/al-fahidi-historical-neighbourhood/)). Travel sources put them at about **2.1–2.4 m wide** ([Middle East Journeys](https://middleeastjourneys.com/dubai-old-town-architecture/)). A study of a former Deira neighbourhood records courtyard houses on **50 × 50 ft (about 15 × 15 m) plots**, with alleys taking **about 13% of the land** ([ResearchGate, Urban Redevelopment Trauma](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/267397270_Urban_Redevelopment_Trauma_The_Story_of_a_Dubai_Neighbourhood)). Houses turn inward to a central courtyard and show few windows to the street. Barjeel wind towers, tall rectangular shafts with side openings, push air into the main room ([alfahidi.org](https://alfahidi.org/architecture-history/)), and the compact alleys shade themselves and channel the wind ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.09365)). A 15 m plot sitting beside a JLT cluster of about 7 ha gives the estimated **5–10× difference in linear grain** between old core and corridor. That figure is an estimate, not a measured one.

The souks are confined to this core and arranged across the water. On the Deira side, the **Gold Souk lines Baniyas Street near the Al Ras abra station**, with the Spice Souk a few minutes' walk away ([Bayut](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/best-souks-in-dubai); [Visit Dubai](https://visitdubai.com/da/articles/ultimate-guide-to-dubai-souks)). The **Textile Souk sits directly across the Creek** in Bur Dubai and is reached by abra ([Expedia](https://www.expedia.co.uk/stories/the-best-souks-in-dubai)). The **Grand Mosque**, with a roughly 70 m minaret and space for about 1,200 worshippers, stands between the Textile Souk and the Dubai Museum ([Bayut](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/grand-mosque-dubai-guide)). Souk-style retail elsewhere, such as Souk Madinat Jumeirah or the Ranches Souk, is resort or community-centre pastiche ([Hidubai](https://focus.hidubai.com/ultimate-guide-to-dubai-souks)). No source gave souk footprint areas or arcade widths.

### Water carves the districts: Creek, canal, marina and palm

The natural Creek runs about 14 km. The 2016 extension carries it through Business Bay and out through the Water Canal to the sea, for about **24 km in total**. The extension averages about **100 m wide and reaches 500 m at its widest** ([Wikipedia Dubai Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_Creek); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/uae%2Fdubai-creek-it-just-got-longer-1.468911)). The Water Canal section is about **3.2 km long and 80–120 m wide**. It crosses under SZR and runs through Safa Park and the Jumeirah villa belt to the Gulf ([Wikipedia Dubai Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_Canal)). In effect, Creek and canal together form a loop that turns the old town, the Trade Centre wall and Downtown into an island. The rule is easy to generate: one organic inlet at the old end of the city that becomes an engineered, tower-lined canal and then turns back to the sea.

Offshore, **Palm Jumeirah** has a roughly 2 km trunk, 17 fronds and an 11 km crescent breakwater, with about 1,400 villas on the fronds ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/nation/dubai/largest-man-made-heritage-island); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/property/palm-jumeirah-to-leave-an-indelible-impression-1.627251)). It adds tens of km of artificial shore. Palm Jebel Ali, still under development, covers about 13.4 km², more than twice the size ([Bayut](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/palm-jumeirah-vs-palm-jebel-ali/)). The frond plots described in listings are long, deep beachfront lots. One frond plot has about 200 m of beach, and a frond-tip plot of 17,466 m² carries permission for seven villas ([Dubizzle](https://dubai.dubizzle.com/property-for-sale/land/2026/7/30/exclusive-200-meter-beachfront-plot-bes-2-116256/); [Property Finder](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/en/plp/buy/land-for-sale-dubai-palm-jumeirah-11398508.html)). That supports a frond layout of one spine road with a single row of villas on each side backing onto the beach, though this layout is an inference.

## Villa belts fill every gap between the tower nodes

Low-rise housing comes in two belts and two street logics. The **coastal belt** of Jumeirah 1–3, Umm Suqeim 1–3 and Al Safa lies between the beach road and SZR ([metropolitan.realestate](https://metropolitan.realestate/umm-suqeim/)). It is an older, mostly non-freehold district on a **rectilinear municipal grid**. Plots there typically run **10,000–15,000 sq ft (930–1,400 m²)**. A corner plot of 15,074 sq ft is zoned "Villa Compound" with permission for three villas ([Property Finder](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/en/plp/buy/land-for-sale-dubai-umm-suqeim-umm-suqeim-2-16071779); [Property Finder corner plot](https://propertyfinder.ae/en/plp/buy/land-for-sale-dubai-umm-suqeim-umm-suqeim-2-12822549.html); [Bayut](https://www.bayut.com/property/details-5356190.html)). The traditional courtyard ideal survives here inside walled grid plots ([ResearchGate](https://researchgate.net/publication/326631039_A_return_to_the_old_landscape_Balancing_physical_planning_ideals_and_cultural_constraints_in_Dubai's_residential_neighborhoods)).

The **inland belt** is made of master-planned, gated, curvilinear estates. Emirates Hills, the Springs and the Meadows lie between SZR and Al Khail behind JLT and the Marina. Dubai Hills and Arabian Ranches lie further out ([apil properties](https://www.apilproperties.com/uae/dubai/communities/emirates-hills); [Propsearch](https://propsearch.ae/dubai/the-meadows)). These estates share a consistent topology. A master community divides into gated sub-communities: Arabian Ranches is reported at 15 to 29 depending on source, with about 4,500 homes ([The National](https://thenationalnews.com/uae/living-in-dubai-s-arabian-ranches-1.1115391)). Each sub-community is laid out with loop roads and cul-de-sacs wrapped around a golf course, a lake chain or a central park. **Dubai Hills Estate** covers about 1,093 ha. It has a 120 ha golf course and 145 ha of total open space, and is planned as two halves, one centred on a park and one on the golf course ([Emaar DHE guide](https://www.emaar.com/en/blog/dubai-hills-estate-guide); [Sotheby's](https://sothebysrealty.ae/the-journal/dubai-hills-master-plan)). **Arabian Ranches** covers about 668 ha and includes a roughly 100 ha golf club ([Dubizzle](https://www.dubizzle.com/blog/property/arabian-ranches-area-overview/); [Emaar](https://www.emaar.com/en/blog/community-review-ar)). Golf therefore takes **about 11–15% of estate land**. Listing language shows how lots are ranked: "full golf course view", "backs onto the Meadows", "cul-de-sac plot".

Listings give a clear **plot-size ladder**. Springs townhouses sit on about 200–390 m² ([Property Finder](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/en/plp/buy/villa-for-sale-dubai-the-springs-springs-5-113699386.html)). Meadows villas sit on about 560–1,110 m² ([Property Finder](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/en/plp/buy/villa-for-sale-dubai-meadows-meadows-1-13756537.html)). A standard Jumeirah Park plot is about 753 m² ([Property Finder](https://propertyfinder.ae/en/plp/buy/land-for-sale-dubai-jumeirah-park-13745749.html)). Palm Signature Villa plots run 13,000–14,000 sq ft, about 1,200–1,300 m² ([Luxhabitat](https://www.luxhabitat.ae/developments/dubai/fronds-signature-villa/)). Emirates Hills plots were originally sold at about 1,100–5,400 m² ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/property/got-dh109m-to-spare-this-dubai-villa-is-waiting-for-you)). Setbacks are fixed plot by plot in each community's Development Control Regulations ([Dubai Building Code 2021](https://u.ae/-/media/Documents-2023/Dubai-Building-Code_English_2021-Edition_compressed-(1).pdf)). The 2025 code update cut the minimum villa setback to **1.5 m** for Emirati family housing ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/dubai-building-code-update)). A low-confidence training source gives a maximum plot coverage of 70% and a 2 m street setback ([FlipHTML5](https://fliphtml5.com/amnxb/stsi)). Collector and local street widths were not found in any accessible source, and the generator uses generic engineering values for them.

## Amenities follow catchments, the spine and walled compounds

Mosques are the densest civic element. Dubai has **over 2,450 mosques and prayer spaces** ([Media Office](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2024/november/21-11/iacad-launches-excellence-in-mosque-management-competition-the-first-of-its-kind-regionally)) for 4.0–4.6 million residents ([WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/c1hd5qo-dubai-population-tops-4580-million-end-2025)), roughly one per 1,700–1,900 people. A further 24–29 open each year ([WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/byh09dx-‘dubai-islamic-affairs-department’-opens-new)). The Dubai Building Code separates small neighbourhood **Awqat** mosques, for the five daily prayers, from larger **Jumaa** Friday mosques ([DBC 2021 copy](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/dubai-building-codeenglish2021-editionakpdf/264939916)). The Dubai Municipality standard is a local mosque on a plot of **at least 1,800 m² serving a 500 m radius**. This comes secondhand, via a walkability study that measured real catchments of 600 m in Arabian Ranches up to 1,000 m in Al Barari ([MDPI Sustainability](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/15/20/15176)). The 2024 openings averaged about 580 worshippers each. Emaar's community mosques range from about 200 to 780 worshippers ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/ramadan/ramadan-gift-dubai-announces-construction-of-55-mosques-expansion-of-friday-sermons-in-english-1.500045780); [Emaar](https://www.emaar.com/en/press-release-listing/emaar-opens-three-new-mosques-during-ramadan-bringing-total-across-its-communities-to-20)). Neighbouring Abu Dhabi uses a stricter 350 m walking limit, with sample plots of 4,242 m² for a Friday mosque and 1,778 m² for a daily mosque ([Abu Dhabi MDR](https://www.dmt.gov.ae/-/media/Project/DMT/DMT/E-Library/10-24/02/Abu-Dhabi-Mosque-Development-Regulations---Volume-1--Planning-En.pdf)). That stricter figure is a reasonable proxy for the dense old town. Each villa community's main mosque sits in its community centre beside retail and a clinic, as in the original Arabian Ranches centre ([Wikipedia: Arabian Ranches](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Arabian_Ranches)).

Non-Muslim worship is the opposite: it is clustered by allocation. Churches and temples stand on land donated by the ruling family ([US State Dept IRF report](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2051727.html)). The historic pair, **St Mary's Catholic and Holy Trinity Anglican**, sit side by side in walled compounds on Oud Metha Road near Bur Dubai ([Dubai As It Used To Be](https://dubaiasitusedtobe.net/ChuckPringleHolyTrinityChurch.shtml)). The newer **Jebel Ali "Corridor of Tolerance"** groups seven churches, a Sikh gurdwara and the 2022 Hindu temple at the far industrial end of the city ([Hinduism Today](https://www.hinduismtoday.com/hpi/?p=18970); [The National](https://thenationalnews.com/uae/heritage/2022/10/04/symbol-of-unity-hindu-temple-that-blends-indian-and-arabic-design-opens-in-dubai)). The first Hindu temple, from 1958, sat on the roof of the Bur Dubai souk ([Hinduism Today](https://www.hinduismtoday.com/hpi/?p=14362)).

Retail is extraordinarily abundant and concentrated on the spine. Dubai has about **1,214 m² of mall space per 1,000 residents** ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/retail/dubai-abu-dhabi-continue-to-be-mall-magnets)). Of that supply, **54% is super-regional, 33% regional and 13% community** ([Knight Frank](https://content.knightfrank.com/research/1267/documents/en/dubai-retail-overview-4708.pdf)). The three spine malls each have a direct metro bridge. The Dubai Mall has its 820 m link. Mall of the Emirates, with 234,470 m² of leasable area, connects to its station by an enclosed overhead walkway ([The Business Year](https://thebusinessyear.com/?p=21633); [Wikipedia](https://Www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mall_of_the_Emirates_(Dubai_Metro))). Ibn Battuta has a 210 m two-storey bridge ([Gulf Business](https://gulfbusiness.com/en/2016/industry/new-link-opens-dubais-ibn-battuta-mall-metro-station/)). Villa communities get small centres instead. The original Arabian Ranches centre has about 20 outlets, and the Ranches Souk has 35 or more units ([metropolitan.realestate](https://metropolitan.realestate/areas/dubai/arabian-ranches/)).

Parks are numerous and small, apart from a few giants. There are about **211–220 public parks totalling about 13 km²** ([Media Office](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2025/august/03-08/dubai-municipality-parks-and-facilities-attract-over-16-million-visitors); [DM](https://www.dm.gov.ae/?p=291563)). **Mushrif covers over 500 ha** of desert forest at the eastern edge, Creek Park 96 ha and Zabeel Park 51 ha ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/lifestyle/family/from-the-palm-to-the-creek-dubai-s-10-best-parks-1.804728); [Planetware](https://planetware.com/united-arab-emirates/best-parks-in-dubai-uae-1-30.htm)). The mean park is about 6 ha, but the distribution is heavily skewed. The city plans **310 new parks by 2040** ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2026/04/05/dubai-to-open-35-parks-this-year/)). Sports venues are spread out rather than clustered. The 17,000-seat Coca-Cola Arena sits in the City Walk lifestyle district near Downtown ([Property Finder](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/blog/coca-cola-arena/)). The 25,000-seat cricket stadium anchors the purpose-built Sports City off Al Khail ([ESPNcricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/other/content/ground/392627.html)). Club grounds of about 8,000 seats, such as Al Wasl's Zabeel Stadium, sit inside older districts ([Bayut](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/sports-stadiums-dubai/)). The coast is mostly private. There are about **158,700 hotel rooms** ([Cavendish Maxwell](https://cavendishmaxwell.com/news/dubai-hotel-market-expands-to-158700-rooms-as-luxury-segment-dominates)), but only **21 km of public beach**, which is planned to grow to 105 km by 2040 ([Media Office](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2023/may/25-05/mohammed-bin-rashid-approves-master-plan-for-dubai-public-beaches)).

### The 2040 plan formalises the polycentric pattern

The **Dubai 2040 Urban Master Plan** designates five urban centres. Three already exist in the corridor: Deira and Bur Dubai, Downtown and Business Bay, and the Marina and JBR. Two are new and inland: Expo City and Dubai Silicon Oasis ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/government/dubai-plan-2040-5-areas-to-see-massive-development)). Its targets are specific. Green and leisure land roughly doubles, hotel land rises 134%, and **60% of the emirate** becomes nature reserves or rural land. Green corridors are to link homes, work and parks for walkers and cyclists ([u.ae](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/strategies-initiatives-and-awards/strategies-plans-and-visions/transport-and-infrastructure/dubai-2040-urban-master-plan)). Phase II adds a **20-minute city** goal, under which 80% of daily needs are reachable on foot or by bike, and puts **55% of residents within 800 m of a mass-transit station**, with density raised around stations ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/government/mohammed-bin-rashid-approves-phase-ii-of-dubai-2040-urban-master-plan-with-20-minute-city-policy-1.92651013)). The official land-use table and green-corridor widths could not be retrieved. For a generator, the plan suggests a "2040 mode": densify around stations, add linear parks, lengthen public beaches and add an inland mid-density centre.

## Compressing Dubai into 600 m means scaling three things differently

The real corridor from Deira to the Marina is about 25–27 km long and about 8 km deep to just past Al Khail. Scaling it uniformly into a 600 m square would take a factor of about 1:45 along the coast. At that factor Burj Khalifa would be 18 m tall and a villa would be smaller than a car. The generator should therefore apply three separate scale factors. **Layout scale** (along the coast about 1:45, inland about 1:14) sets where districts sit, and it preserves the order and adjacency of nodes, gaps, bands and waterways. **Spacing scale** (about 1:5–1:12) sets the repeat interval of stations, interchanges and mosque catchments. The aim is for the player to meet the right *number of repetitions*: three or four metro stations, about a dozen mosques. **Object scale** (about 1:1–1:5) sets the size of buildings, plots and streets. It keeps lanes, villas and podiums at near-human scale and shrinks towers enough that the skyline fits. The inland compression is gentler than the along-coast compression because Dubai is a ribbon and the game map is a square. If the game can afford it, running the corridor diagonally across the map buys about 850 m of length and reduces the distortion.

## Conclusion

The most useful insight for a generator is that Dubai's legibility comes from **contrast at boundaries**, not gradients. Towers stop abruptly at villa walls. The 15 m courtyard grain of the old town sits against 7 ha tower clusters. A 16-lane spine is crossed only at a few interchanges and station bridges. A generator that blends land uses smoothly will produce a generic city; one that hard-switches typology at band and node edges will read as Dubai. The second insight is that the city is assembled from a small set of repeatable modules: the podium-with-towers, the tower ring around a lake, the JLT-style tri-tower cluster on a lake chain, the gated loop estate around a golf course, the walled villa compound in a grid, and the creek-side souk pair. Each module carries its own amenity package of mosque, community retail and pocket park. That modularity makes Dubai unusually well suited to rule-based generation.

Three areas remain weakly evidenced and should be tuned by eye rather than treated as fact. These are the spacings between the inland highways, street and right-of-way widths below arterial level, and tower spacing and podium setbacks. Souk dimensions and wind-tower sizes are likewise unsourced. The rules below mark these as design values.

## Generator rules for a 600 m Dubai-style city

**Frame and scale conventions.** The map is a 600 × 600 m square. The *x* axis runs along the coast from the old-town end (x = 0, the "north-east") to the marina end (x = 600). *d* is the distance inland from the shoreline. A sea strip of 60 m lies seaward of the shoreline, so the land is 600 × 540 m. Each parameter is followed by the real-world value it is scaled from, in brackets. "Design value" marks a parameter that has no sourced real basis.

### A. Bands and the spine

1. **Band order (hard constraint).** Generate land in strict coast-parallel bands, in this order: beach at d 0–20 m; a coastal road at d ≈ 30 m with an 18 m right-of-way; a coastal villa belt at d 40–125 m; the spine highway centred at d = 150 m; an inland mixed band at d 175–380 m; a second arterial at d ≈ 400 m; and an outer suburb/sports band at d 420–540 m. [Real: coast, then Jumeirah Beach Rd at about 0.3–1 km, villa belt about 1.5–2 km deep, SZR at about 2 km, then Al Khail at about 5–7 km. All estimates except SZR.]
2. **Spine cross-section.** Give the spine 3–4 lanes each way at 3.5 m, an 8–10 m median that carries the elevated metro viaduct, and a 2-lane service road on each side. Total right-of-way is 44–50 m. [Real: SZR has 7–8 lanes each way; ROW of about 100–120 m is estimated. Halved lane count and about 1:2.4 width so it stays drivable but still reads as a barrier.]
3. **Spine as barrier.** No at-grade crossings. The spine is crossed only at 3 grade-separated interchanges, at x ≈ 120, 330 and 540 (every 180–220 m), and at pedestrian bridges at each metro station. [Real: numbered interchanges, about every 2–5 km (estimate). SZR "cuts the city in two."]
4. **Perpendicular connectors.** From each interchange, run one 4-lane connector (20 m ROW) from the coastal road to the second arterial. Add one more connector between each pair of interchanges that stops at the spine service road. [Real: coast-to-inland streets crossing the bands at interchanges. Design value.]
5. **Second arterial.** At d ≈ 400 m, a 2+2-lane road with a landscaped median (24–28 m ROW), crossing every connector at grade or by roundabout. [Real: Al Khail Rd / E311 at 6 lanes each way, scaled down because it is secondary.]

### B. Node sequence along the spine

6. **Node order along x.** Old town at x 0–120; tower wall at x 110–185; Downtown + canal district at x 180–330; mid-rise gap at x 330–450; marina super-node at x 450–600. [Real: Deira/Bur Dubai at km 0, Trade Centre/DIFC at km 4–7, Downtown/Business Bay at km 7–10, gap at km 10–23, Marina/JLT at km 23–27. Map estimates. The gap is compressed hardest, about 1:100, to give nodes room.]
7. **Minimum gap.** Keep at least 100 m of x-distance between the Downtown node and the marina super-node with no building over 30 m, except a single mall node. [Real: 15–20 km of predominantly low/mid-rise fabric between super-nodes.]
8. **Tower budget.** Generate 45–55 "tall" buildings (≥ 30 m in game), allocated in proportion to the real clusters. With 50 towers, that gives Downtown + canal district 14, marina + beachfront wall 14, the lake-tower cluster across the spine 10, the spine wall 9, and old-town fringe 3. [Real: CTBUH cluster counts of buildings ≥100 m, which are 88 / 87 / 61 / 55, plus a small Deira count. 100 m scaled at 1:3.3 gives 30 m.]
9. **Straddling super-node.** The marina node occupies both spine frontages: marina and beachfront on the sea side (d 20–130), lake towers on the inland side (d 175–310). [Real: Marina/JBR seaward of SZR, JLT directly inland.]
10. **Height field.** Tower height is H = max over nodes of Peak·exp(−r²/2σ²), plus or minus 15% noise. Use these node parameters. Downtown: one landmark at 160–180 m, and a ring of 50–90 m with σ = 65 m. Canal district: 35–70 m, σ = 60 m. Spine wall: 45–80 m, using an elongated kernel 150 m along x by 25 m across, hugging both spine frontages. Marina: 55–105 m, σ = 60 m. Lake towers: 45–85 m. Old-town fringe: 20–35 m. [Real: Burj Khalifa 828 m, Downtown ring 200–400 m, Business Bay 150–300 m, Emirates Towers 333 m, Marina 250–427 m, JLT 150–350 m, Deira 80–150 m. Object scale about 1:3.3–1:4.8. σ is scaled from about 1 km at about 1:15.]
11. **Hard height cliffs.** Clamp the height field to the villa cap (rule 26) wherever the band mask says "villa". The drop from towers to villas must happen within one block (25–60 m), never across a gradient wider than 60 m. [Real: towers fall to 1–2 storeys within about 200–500 m at JLT/Emirates Hills and Downtown/Jumeirah. Inferred.]
12. **Mid-rise fill.** In the inland mixed band between nodes, build 3–8 storeys (10–28 m) on a regular grid. Mix in 20–30% low industrial sheds (6–10 m) on the far side, toward the second arterial. [Real: Al Barsha/Tecom/JVC at about 4–20 storeys and Al Quoz industrial. Storey ranges are inferred.]

### C. Tower district modules

13. **Podium-and-tower module.** The podium is 2–4 levels (10–16 m) and covers 60–80% of its superblock. Ground floor is retail, upper podium levels are parking. It carries 1–3 towers with a footprint of 18–28 m square and an aspect ratio (height ÷ width) of at least 2.5. Towers sit at least 15 m apart. [Real: JBR B+G+M+P+UP stack, about 15–25 m; 1–3 towers per podium; towers of 40–55 floors. Spacing and coverage are design values.]
14. **Downtown ring module.** An elliptical boulevard loop of 180 × 140 m (perimeter about 500 m). Its seaward edge is the spine service road. Towers line both sides of the loop. At the centre place a lake of 0.15–0.25 ha (about 6–10% of the enclosed area). Put the landmark supertall on one edge of the lake and the super-regional mall on another edge. [Real: 3.5 km boulevard around 200 ha; 12 ha Burj Lake, about 6%; Burj Khalifa and Dubai Mall beside the lake.]
15. **Canal district module.** A straight-to-gently-curving canal, 18–22 m wide, parallel to the coast at d ≈ 335 m from x 120 to 330. Tower plots of 35–50 m line both banks behind a 6 m promenade. [Real: Business Bay at about 4.4 km² on both canal banks, canal about 100 m wide; south of Downtown between SZR and Al Khail.]
16. **Marina module.** An enclosed canal 18–22 m wide and 140–160 m long, parallel to the coast at d ≈ 90 m, open to the sea at its x = 600 end. Promenades and towers line both banks. [Real: 3–4 km canal, at least 70 m wide, about 11 km of water frontage, about 200 high-rises.]
17. **Beachfront wall module.** On the seaward side of the marina, at d 20–45 m, build a continuous 2-level podium about 120 m long with a ground-level retail promenade facing the sea. Put 6–8 slab towers on top in clusters of 2–3. [Real: JBR, a 1.7 km strip of 36 residential towers of 39–54 storeys plus 4 hotel towers on "The Walk", 2 levels and 311 outlets.]
18. **Lake-tower cluster module.** Opposite the marina across the spine, place 2 artificial lakes of 30–45 m chained parallel to the spine. Ring them with 4–5 clusters. Each cluster is a podium plinth of about 45 × 45 m (square or triangular) carrying 3 towers. [Real: JLT, 26 clusters × 3 towers around 3–4 lakes, about 7 ha per cluster.]

### D. Water

19. **Creek.** An organic inlet 25–40 m wide (widen to 50 m at one bend) enters from the sea at x ≈ 40. It curves inland through the old town and joins the canal district canal at about (x 130, d 335). [Real: natural Creek about 14 km; extension about 100 m wide, up to 500 m.]
20. **Return canal.** At x ≈ 330, the canal turns seaward as a 15–18 m engineered canal. It passes under the spine by bridge and runs through the coastal villa belt to the sea. It marks the boundary between the Downtown node and the gap. [Real: Water Canal, 3.2 km long and 80–120 m wide, through Safa Park and Jumeirah to the Gulf.]
21. **Optional palm.** If the map is extended offshore near x 400–470, add a palm island. It has a 120–150 m trunk with a central spine road and 7–9 fronds of 50–70 m. Each frond is a single spine road with one row of villa plots per side, each with private beach frontage. Larger plots go at the frond tips. A crescent breakwater carries 2–3 resort hotels. [Real: Palm Jumeirah, a 2 km trunk, 17 fronds, an 11 km crescent and about 1,400 villas. Frond layout inferred from listings.]

### E. Old town

22. **Old-town street network.** Both creek banks at x 0–120, d 20–200: an irregular organic network. Sikka lanes are 2.5–3 m wide (widened from the real width for player movement), and 20–30% of them dead-end. Lanes take 12–15% of the land. One or two souk spines, 4–6 m wide, are roofed with timber or palm-frond arcades. [Real: sikkas about 2.1–2.4 m; alleys about 13% of land. Souk width is a design value.]
23. **Courtyard houses.** Plots are 12–15 m square with 80–85% built cover. Houses are 1–2 storeys (4–8 m) with an inward courtyard of 25–35% of the plot and few street windows. 40–60% of houses get a wind tower 3 m square rising 4–6 m above the roof. [Real: 15 × 15 m Deira plots; barjeel wind towers. Courtyard share and tower size are design values.]
24. **Souk pair and abra.** Place a gold/spice souk cluster on the old-town bank (the side toward x = 0) and a textile souk on the opposite bank, directly facing each other within 60 m of the creek mouth. Put 2–3 abra (water-taxi) piers between them. The grand mosque (minaret 25–30 m) goes beside the textile souk. [Real: Deira Gold and Spice Souks at the Al Ras abra; Textile Souk opposite; Grand Mosque with a 70 m minaret between the souk and the museum.]
25. **Mid-rise collar.** Ring the courtyard core with a regular grid of 3–6 storey (10–20 m) commercial blocks, 30–45 m on a side. Add the 3 old-town towers (rule 8) along the creek front on the old-town bank. [Real: 1970s–90s mid-rise around the core and early Deira towers. Storey range inferred.]

### F. Villa belts

26. **Villa height cap.** All villa land is 1–2 storeys plus an optional rooftop room, 7–10 m, at object scale 1:1. [Real: 1–2 storey villas; rooftop floors permitted with an extra 1.5 m setback.]
27. **Coastal villa grid.** In the coastal belt between nodes, use an orthogonal grid of blocks about 48 m deep (2 rows of back-to-back plots) and 100–130 m long. Local streets are 10–12 m. Plots are about 20 × 24 m. 15% are corner or double plots at about 2× area holding 2–3 villas. Every plot gets a perimeter wall of 2–2.5 m. [Real: Jumeirah/Umm Suqeim plots of 930–1,400 m², "villa compound" plots with 1–3 villas. Wall height and street widths are design values.]
28. **Gated estates.** Inland, in the outer band and behind the lake towers, generate master-planned estates of 1.5–3 ha. Each has 1–2 gates and is split into 3–5 sub-communities connected by a curvilinear loop road (14–16 m ROW). Cul-de-sacs (8–10 m carriageway, 10–12 m turning radius) branch off the loop. [Real: master community, then 15–30 gated sub-communities, then loops and cul-de-sacs. Widths are generic engineering values.]
29. **Central amenity per estate.** Give each estate one golf/lake/park core covering 11–15% of its area. The premium plot row faces it directly. [Real: golf about 11–15% of Dubai Hills and Arabian Ranches; plots sold as "full golf course view" or "backs onto".]
30. **Plot-size ladder** (choose a tier per sub-community). Townhouse: 150–250 m² with 8–10 m frontage [real 200–400 m²]. Mid villa: 300–550 m² with 15–20 m frontage [real 550–1,100 m²]. Mansion: 900–1,500 m² with 30–40 m frontage, golf-front only [real 1,100–5,400 m²]. For mixing, use roughly 30% townhouse, 55% mid villa and 15% mansion (design value).
31. **Setbacks and coverage.** Front setback 3–5 m, side setback at least 1.5 m, coverage 40–70%. [Real: 1.5 m minimum villa setback (2025 code); about 70% maximum coverage and 2 m street setback (low confidence).]

### G. Amenities

32. **Daily mosques.** Place small mosques by Poisson-disc sampling over residential land with a 90–110 m catchment radius (use 60–70 m in the old town). This yields roughly 10–14 mosques. Plot 600–900 m²; minaret 12–20 m. [Real: Dubai Municipality local mosque on at least 1,800 m² with a 500 m radius; Abu Dhabi 350 m walking limit; about 1 mosque per 1,700–1,900 residents.]
33. **Friday mosques.** One per district or estate centre, 2–3 in total, plus the grand mosque. Plot 1,500–2,000 m²; dome and 1–2 minarets of 20–30 m. Always co-locate with community retail and a clinic. [Real: Jumaa mosques; Abu Dhabi Friday-mosque sample plot of 4,242 m²; Arabian Ranches centre with mosque, retail and clinic.]
34. **Multi-faith compound.** Exactly one walled compound of 0.3–0.5 ha holding 3–5 church or temple buildings on adjacent plots. Place it on the inland fringe of the old town near the spine, or at the far industrial edge. Never scatter these buildings individually. [Real: Oud Metha's adjacent St Mary's and Holy Trinity; Jebel Ali's 9-shrine "Corridor of Tolerance"; roughly 100+ mosques per church or temple.]
35. **Super-regional malls.** Place 2 malls (a 3rd is optional at x ≈ 590), each 2–3 storeys (16–24 m) with a footprint of 9,000–14,000 m², directly against the spine. One goes in the Downtown ring (rule 14) and one in the gap at x ≈ 390 on the inland frontage. Each connects to its metro station by an enclosed elevated bridge of 20–60 m. [Real: Dubai Mall about 350,000 m² GLA with an 820 m link; Mall of the Emirates 234,470 m² GLA with an overhead walkway; Ibn Battuta with a 210 m bridge; spacing about 10–13 km.]
36. **Community retail.** Each estate centre and each villa-grid district gets a 1–2 storey "souk"-style centre of 800–1,500 m² footprint with 15–30 units, next to its Friday mosque. Mall-type retail should total 60–70% of retail floor area and community retail 10–15%. [Real: Arabian Ranches centre about 20 outlets, Ranches Souk 35+; 54% super-regional, 33% regional, 13% community.]
37. **Park hierarchy.** One city park of 1.2–2 ha on a creek bank at the old-town end [real: Creek Park 96 ha, Zabeel 51 ha]. One linear canal-side park of 0.5–0.8 ha along the return canal in the villa belt [real: Safa Park on the Water Canal]. One community park of 0.2–0.5 ha at the centre of each estate or villa district. Pocket parks of 200–800 m² for roughly every 4 villa blocks. Target 8–13% of land as green [real: 145 ha of 1,093 ha in Dubai Hills, about 13%]. Optionally add a desert-forest patch at an inland corner [real: Mushrif, over 500 ha].
38. **Sports venues.** One indoor arena (footprint about 60 × 70 m, 25–30 m tall) in a lifestyle pocket on the coast side of the spine next to the return canal (x 330–380). One sports-city cluster beyond the second arterial (x 300–440, d 420–540) containing a stadium bowl (about 90 × 110 m) with training pitches. One small club ground (about 60 × 80 m) in the old-town mid-rise collar. [Real: Coca-Cola Arena, 17k seats, at City Walk; 25k cricket stadium in Sports City off Al Khail; club grounds of about 8k seats in older districts. Footprints are design values.]
39. **Coastline allocation.** Public beach parks of 60–120 m each should total 25–35% of the shoreline. Use the rest for resort hotels with private beaches and the beachfront wall. Hotels also appear at the Downtown ring and the old-town creek front. [Real: 21 km of public beach today, mostly private hotel frontage, about 158,700 hotel rooms.]

### H. Transit

40. **Main metro line.** An elevated viaduct in the spine median, at a deck height of 10–12 m, along the full x extent. Place 4 stations at x ≈ 100, 240, 390 and 530 (spacing 130–150 m). Each station has a pedestrian bridge over both carriageways. [Real: Red Line, about 67 km and 35 stations, about 1.6–1.9 km apart, elevated along SZR.]
41. **Old-town loop.** A second line that drops underground and crosses beneath the creek, with 2 stations in the old town about 80–100 m apart. It meets the main line at the x ≈ 100 station. [Real: Green Line, 22.5 km and 20 stations about 1.1 km apart, looping through Deira and Bur Dubai under the Creek.]
42. **Marina tram.** A surface tram loop through the marina and beachfront wall with 3 stops 70–90 m apart, connecting to the x ≈ 530 metro station. [Real: tram, about 10.6 km and 11 stops, Al Sufouh to Marina to JBR, about 1 km apart.]
43. **Transit-oriented density.** At least 55% of residential floor area must lie within 100 m of a metro or tram stop. Multiply the allowed height by 1.3 within 60 m of a station. [Real: 2040 target of 55% of residents within 800 m of mass transit, with density raised around stations.]

### I. 2040 mode and validation

44. **2040 toggle.** When enabled, apply all of the following. Add 1–2 linear green corridors, 12–18 m wide, linking each estate to the nearest node and to the city park. Raise the public-beach share toward 50%. Double pocket-park frequency. Add one inland mid-density centre (6–12 storeys) beyond the second arterial at the old-town end. [Real: 2040 plan, with five centres including 2 new inland ones; green and leisure land doubled; beaches 21 km to 105 km; green corridors. Corridor width is a design value.]
45. **Validation checks** (reject and reseed if any fail). (a) At least 3 discrete tall clusters separated by low gaps. (b) No villa within 25 m of a tower over 45 m unless separated by a road or wall. (c) Every residential plot within one catchment radius of a mosque. (d) Every estate has a gate, a Friday mosque, retail and a central amenity. (e) No churches or temples outside the compound. (f) The spine is crossed only at interchanges and station bridges. (g) The souk pair faces across the creek. [These encode the polycentric, hard-edged, catchment-driven patterns documented above.]
