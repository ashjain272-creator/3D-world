# Dubai macro-scale urban structure: the linear coastal corridor and how its districts are arranged

Research note: this session could not open web pages directly. Wikipedia, dm.gov.ae and thenationalnews.com were all blocked, so every citation below comes from search-engine summaries of the linked pages. The summaries were checked against each other where possible. Numbers marked "approx." in the Inferences sections are map-based estimates from general geographic knowledge. They are not cited facts, and they should be read as starting values for the generator to tune.

## 1. The city as a linear corridor parallel to the coast (coast, SZR, inland roads, spacing)

### Takeaway
Dubai is a coastal ribbon city that runs NE–SW. The coast, Jumeirah Beach Road, Sheikh Zayed Road (E11), Al Khail Road (E44 west section), Mohammed bin Zayed Road (E311) and Emirates Road (E611) form roughly parallel bands. Each band sits further inland and is newer and more suburban. Between the coast and SZR, about 2 km apart, lies a continuous low-rise villa strip. SZR itself carries the tower nodes.

### Cited Findings
- SZR runs parallel to the coastline about 2 km inland. Between the two lies the city's largest continuous urban development, mostly low-rise residential, which becomes denser and more commercial toward the centre. Red Line metro stations sit about 1 mile (~1.6 km) apart at activity nodes such as malls and business centres. — [Planetizen](https://www.planetizen.com/node/41919)
- SZR runs more than 70 km through the city, links most key destinations, and "cuts the city in two". — [Transform Transport](https://transformtransport.org/media/articles/sheikh-zayed-road/)
- Some guides describe the SZR corridor as "one linear urban strip" with the Red Line running alongside. These are marketing sources. — [Binayah](https://www.binayah.com/dubai-areas/sheikh-zayed-road-properties-for-sale-rent/)
- Development forms a ribbon along the Arabian Gulf coastal strip. The pace, scale and complexity of Dubai's coastal reclamation are called "unprecedented". — [Lancaster Univ., "Developing the desert"](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/68671/1/Accepted_manuscript.pdf)
- A space-syntax study models about 1,100 km² of metropolitan Dubai and describes a north–south linear pattern along the coast. Because of this linear coastal growth, the historic Deira core has limited accessibility to the enlarged metro region. — [Springer, Hovsepian et al.](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-11306-1_12)
- Dubai has become a "coastal linear city". Together, Dubai, Sharjah and Ajman form a conurbation more than 80 km long along the Gulf coast. — [ResearchGate, "The Boom"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317584226_The_Boom_Population_and_Urban_Growth_of_Dubai_City)
- Between 1972 and 2011, growth went mainly toward the west coast (the Palm area), and more than 11 km² of sea was converted to built-up land. — [GISRUK 2012](https://www.geos.ed.ac.uk/~gisteac/proceedingsonline/GISRUK2012/Papers/presentation-65.pdf)
- E311 (Sheikh Mohammed bin Zayed Road) runs parallel to E11 and passes through urban areas. E611 (Emirates Road) serves as an outer bypass for suburban communities in outer Dubai. — [Wikipedia E611 via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E_611_road_(United_Arab_Emirates)); [Property Finder](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/blog/highways/)
- Within Dubai, E44 is named Al Khail Road between E311 and D68. It passes Dubai Sports City, Jumeirah Village Triangle and JVC, and has junctions at Al Meydan St (D69) and Umm Suqeim St (D63). — [Wikipedia E44 via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E_44_road_(United_Arab_Emirates)); [Property Finder](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/blog/highways/)
- Emirates Hills (Emirates Living) lies between SZR and Al Khail Road. The Springs is reached from both Al Khail Road and SZR. — [apil properties](https://www.apilproperties.com/uae/dubai/communities/emirates-hills); [Propsearch](https://propsearch.ae/dubai/the-meadows)
- One search summary said E611 "starts at Wafi Mall junction". That is probably a confusion with another road (Wafi is in the old core near Oud Metha), so do not use it.

### Inferences
- Use band offsets measured perpendicular to the coast (approx., SW half of the city around Al Barsha/Marina). Coast and beach at 0 km. Jumeirah Beach Road / Al Wasl Road at 0.3–1 km. SZR at about 2 km (cited). Al Khail Road at about 5–7 km. E311 at about 13–16 km. E611 at about 20–25 km. Near the old core the bands converge: SZR lies only about 1.5–2 km from the coast at Trade Centre, and the roads curve to wrap around the Creek.
- Profile from coast to inland: [beach/hotels or reclaimed islands] → [low-rise villas, Jumeirah/Umm Suqeim/Al Safa] → [SZR, with towers in nodes along both frontages] → [mixed mid-rise/industrial/villa estates: Al Quoz, Al Barsha, Emirates Living, Dubai Hills, JVC] → [Al Khail Road] → [master-planned suburbs and logistics: Arabian Ranches, Motor City, Sports City, Al Quoz industrial] → [E311/E611] → [desert or new satellite centres: Expo City, DSO, Al Maktoum airport].
- For generation, a coast-parallel "spine" (SZR) with a Red Line metro alongside and stations every ~1.6 km is a defensible rule. Connector streets run perpendicular from coast to inland and cross the bands at interchanges.

### Gaps
- No source gave measured distances between SZR, Al Khail, E311 and E611. The offsets above are approximate.
- The official length of the Dubai section of each road could not be confirmed because sources disagree, e.g. E311 at 140 km vs 558 km for the whole route.

## 2. High-rise clusters: locations, sizes, spacing (nodes vs continuous wall)

### Takeaway
CTBUH treats Dubai as a "tall, polycentric city" with six discrete tower clusters (309 buildings ≥100 m) strung along SZR. The two dominant clusters are Downtown+Business Bay at the NE end of the new corridor and Marina+JLT at the SW end, about 16–22 km apart. A smaller continuous strip runs along SZR from Trade Centre to DIFC, and an older lower cluster sits in Deira. The stretches between nodes (Al Wasl/Al Quoz/Al Barsha/Umm Suqeim) are much lower.

### Cited Findings
- CTBUH counts six clusters with 309 buildings ≥100 m. Downtown Dubai + Business Bay has 88, Dubai Marina 87, Jumeirah Lakes Towers 61, and Al Satwa/DIFC (the SZR strip) 55. A cluster is defined as at least five buildings ≥100 m. A city is polycentric if it has three or more clusters. — [CTBUH, Safarik, "The Tall, Polycentric City"](https://global.ctbuh.org/resources/papers/3816-Safarik_PolycentricCities.pdf)
- CTBUH calls SZR the unifying element at whole-city scale and the "front door" to the Marina. Deira, the original trading core, is named as one of the earliest tall clusters. — [CTBUH paper](https://global.ctbuh.org/resources/papers/download/3816-the-tall-polycentric-city-dubai-and-the-future-of-vertical-urbanism.pdf)
- Britannica describes the new centre as a line of skyscrapers along SZR, with newer clusters further west around a man-made marina and lakes. — [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Dubai-United-Arab-Emirates)
- The SZR "Trade Centre" locality starts at Trade Centre Roundabout and ends at the 2nd Interchange (Defence Roundabout). It is almost entirely high-rise. One article calls it "the 3-km stretch". Emirates Towers on SZR rises to 333 m. — [Wikipedia Trade Centre 1 via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_Centre_1); [Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/points-of-interest/emirates-towers/1322222)
- Dubai has 73 completed or topped-out buildings ≥200 m. Another source counts 28 over 300 m, the most of any city, though that source is not CTBUH-attributed. — [Wikipedia list via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Dubai); [Reference.org](https://reference.org/facts/list_of_tallest_buildings_in_the_united_arab_emirates/hmFlMRs2)
- Distances: Marina to Downtown is about 16 km straight-line or about 22 km by road. DIFC to Downtown is about 3.6 km and DIFC to Marina about 23.9 km by road. — [geodatos](https://www.geodatos.net/en/distances/cities/united-arab-emirates/dubai-rgn); [apil properties](https://apilproperties.com/question/communities/how-far-is-the-addresss-dubai-marina-from-downtown-dubai); [Dubizzle](https://www.dubizzle.com/blog/property/renting-dubai-marina-vs-downtown-dubai/)
- Dubai Marina is an artificial canal city built along a 3 km stretch of shoreline. — [Wikipedia Dubai Marina via search](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_Marina)
- Sources disagree on the JLT tower count ("nearly 100" vs 80) and on its lakes (four vs three). — [arrivalguides](https://www.arrivalguides.com/en/Travelguide/DUBAI/thecity/jumeirah-lakes-towers-129406); [Property Finder](https://propertyfinder.ae/en/area-insights/dubai/jumeirah-lake-towers)

### Inferences
- Approx. order and spacing of nodes along SZR from NE to SW: Deira/Bur Dubai old core (km 0, NE of the Creek) → Trade Centre/SZR strip + DIFC (km ~4–7) → Downtown/Burj Khalifa + Business Bay (km ~7–10, inland side of SZR around the canal) → mid-rise gap (Al Quoz/Al Wasl/Al Safa/Al Barsha, km ~10–20, with a smaller node at Mall of the Emirates/Al Barsha) → Media City/Internet City/Knowledge Village/Tecom (km ~20–23, low-to-mid rise with some towers) → Marina/JBR (coast side) facing JLT (inland side) (km ~23–27) → Jebel Ali/port/free zone (km 30+, low industrial). Treat these as approximate.
- Generation rule: place 4–6 tower nodes along the spine. Make two "super-nodes" (≈90 buildings ≥100 m each) at roughly 1/3 and 3/4 along the corridor, separated by 15–20 km of predominantly low/mid-rise fabric. The secondary nodes are a linear wall (~3 km long, ~1–2 blocks deep on each side of SZR) and an older low node of 55–60 towers in the historic core. Marina/JLT straddle SZR: Marina sits on the sea side and JLT on the inland side, so one node occupies both frontages.
- Nodes are discrete: the gaps between them are low. Only the Trade Centre–DIFC strip behaves like a continuous "wall".

### Gaps
- The exact count of towers ≥150 m per cluster was not available because the CTBUH table columns could not be read.
- No measured areas or footprints were found for Downtown, Business Bay or JLT.

## 3. Old town (Deira, Bur Dubai, Al Fahidi, Creek souks) vs the modern corridor

### Takeaway
The old core straddles Dubai Creek at the NE end of the corridor. It is a fine-grained, low-rise, dense fabric of narrow shaded lanes (sikkas) and courtyard houses with wind towers, surrounded by mid-rise commercial blocks. This contrasts with the coarse superblocks and towers along SZR. Deira has some older towers but is far lower than the new nodes.

### Cited Findings
- Al Fahidi (Al Bastakiya) sits on the south (Bur Dubai) bank of the Creek and dates from the 1890s. In its prime it had about 60 housing units, mostly separated by narrow, winding lanes. — [Wikipedia Al Bastakiya via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Bastakiya)
- Its narrow lanes (sikkas) give shade and air movement. Wind towers (barjeel) are tall rectangular towers with side openings that channel air into the main room. Street-facing windows are few, for privacy. Over 50 restored buildings survive; sources disagree on the count. These are tourism sources. — [alfahidi.org](https://alfahidi.org/architecture-history/); [guideofdubai](https://www.guideofdubai.com/en/blog/al-fahidi-historical-neighborhood-dubais-living-heritage)
- Al Seef, the nearby newer heritage-style development, departs from the traditional narrow sikka alleys. — [Wikipedia Al Seef via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Seef)
- Deira's historic core is poorly connected to the enlarged metropolis because of linear coastal growth. — [Springer space-syntax study](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-11306-1_12)
- Deira is one of the earliest tall-building clusters. — [CTBUH](https://global.ctbuh.org/resources/papers/3816-Safarik_PolycentricCities.pdf)
- The natural Creek is about 14 km long; with the 2016 extension through Business Bay and the Water Canal, the total is about 24 km. — [Wikipedia Dubai Creek via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_Creek)

### Inferences
- Generation rule: the old town is an irregular organic street network on both banks of a winding inlet at one end of the corridor. Lanes are 2–4 m wide, plots small, and buildings 1–3 storeys with wind towers and courtyards. Souks line the Creek banks, with abra crossing points between banks. This core is ringed by a mid-rise belt (approx. 4–12 storeys) of 1970s–90s commercial blocks on a regular grid, with a few older towers along the Creek front in Deira.
- The old town grain is roughly 5–10× finer than the SZR superblock grain. This is an estimate, not a cited figure.

### Gaps
- No sourced figures for typical Deira or Bur Dubai building heights (e.g. "G+4") or densities.
- No width for the natural Creek. The extension averages about 100 m wide and reaches about 500 m at its widest (see section 5).

## 4. Villa belts relative to towers and coast

### Takeaway
Villa land falls in two belts. The first is a coastal belt between the beach and SZR: Jumeirah 1–3, Umm Suqeim 1–3, Al Safa and Al Manara, running from about 1 km SW of the Trade Centre strip to the Burj Al Arab area. The second is an inland belt between SZR and Al Khail Road and beyond: Emirates Hills/Springs/Meadows/Lakes behind JLT/Marina, Al Barsha South, Dubai Hills, and further out Arabian Ranches. Tower nodes are often bordered directly by villa land.

### Cited Findings
- Umm Suqeim is a coastal area between Jumeirah Beach Road and SZR, in western Dubai along the Jumeirah Beach coastline. Its villas are mostly in Umm Suqeim 1–3, and much of the area is non-freehold. — [metropolitan.realestate](https://metropolitan.realestate/umm-suqeim/); [Wikipedia Umm Suqeim via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umm_Suqeim)
- Planetizen says the land between SZR and the coast (about 2 km wide) is mostly low-rise residential. — [Planetizen](https://www.planetizen.com/node/41919)
- Emirates Hills, part of Emirates Living, lies between SZR and Al Khail Road, with access to Dubai Marina and JLT. The Meadows has over 1,800 villas along tree-lined streets and lakes. The Springs is reachable from Al Khail Road and SZR. — [apil properties](https://www.apilproperties.com/uae/dubai/communities/emirates-hills); [Propsearch](https://propsearch.ae/dubai/the-meadows)
- One guide places Dubai Hills Estate between Umm Suqeim (i.e. inland of it) and Al Khail Road. — [breakingac villa guide](https://breakingac.com/news/2024/oct/16/a-guide-to-dubais-top-villa-communities/)

### Inferences
- Emirates Hills, the Springs, the Meadows and the Lakes sit immediately inland of JLT across SZR. This gives a sharp transition from roughly 40–80 storey towers to 1–2 storey villas within about 0.5–1 km. Similarly, the Jumeirah villa belt sits directly coastward of the SZR Trade Centre strip and Downtown.
- Generation rule: on the coast side of the spine, fill the gap between tower nodes with a villa belt about 1.5–2 km deep. Inland, lay out curvilinear gated villa estates with artificial lakes or golf courses (Emirates Hills golf course, Meadows/Springs lakes) between SZR and the 2nd parallel road, and more estates beyond it (Arabian Ranches near E311). Estates are typically a few km² each, approx.

### Gaps
- No sourced sizes for Arabian Ranches, Dubai Hills, Al Barsha South or Al Safa, and no exact belt widths.

## 5. Waterfront elements: Creek, Water Canal, Business Bay, Marina, Palms, beaches

### Takeaway
Water organises the districts. The Creek defines the old core, and its 2016 extension forms a continuous waterway (Creek → Business Bay → Water Canal) that cuts across SZR to the sea and creates a canal-front tower district. The Marina is a 3 km inland canal that forms its own node. The Palms are offshore frond islands that add tens of km of artificial shore. Public beaches are short today and are planned to grow about 5×.

### Cited Findings
- The Dubai Water Canal is about 3.2 km long and 80–120 m wide. It runs from Business Bay through Safa Park and Jumeirah to the Gulf. The wider project covers 9 km from Ras Al Khor through Business Bay to SZR. Another source calls it a 6.4 km waterfront. — [Wikipedia Dubai Canal via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_Canal); [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/mohammed-bin-rashid-inaugurates-the-dubai-water-canal-koeznwzo); [Khaleej Times](https://khaleejtimes.com/uae/dubai-water-canal-facts-and-figures-every-uae-resident-should-be-proud-of)
- The Creek is about 24 km in total: 14 km natural plus about 13 km of extension. The extension averages 100 m wide and is up to 500 m at its widest. — [Wikipedia Dubai Creek via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_Creek); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/uae%2Fdubai-creek-it-just-got-longer-1.468911)
- Dubai Marina is an artificial canal city along about 3 km of shoreline. — [Wikipedia via search](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_Marina)
- Palm Jumeirah covers about 5.6 km² and measures about 3.5 × 3.5 mi (about 5.6 km per side), with 17 fronds. Its crescent breakwater is about 7 mi (about 11 km) long. It added roughly 56 km, or by another estimate about 78 km, of coastline. — [Tucson.com](https://tucson.com/news/article_4517f25f-ad53-5ede-8a32-241d34241193.html); [wikiwand Palm Islands](https://wikiwand.com/en/articles/Palm_Islands); [Sotheby's](https://sothebysrealty.ae/the-journal/palm-jebel-ali-vs-palm-jumeirah)
- Palm Jebel Ali covers about 13.4 km², more than twice Palm Jumeirah. It adds about 110 km of coast (120 km per Bayut), and its crescent is about 16 km. It is under development. — [Bayut](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/palm-jumeirah-vs-palm-jebel-ali/); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/trends/photo/palm-jebel-ali-dubais-new-mega-island-twice-the-size-of-palm-jumeirah-496115-2025-09-29)
- Public beaches are planned to expand from 21 km to 105 km by 2040, an increase of up to 400%. — [u.ae](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/strategies-initiatives-and-awards/strategies-plans-and-visions/transport-and-infrastructure/dubai-2040-urban-master-plan); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/government/dubai-2040-urban-plan-60-of-dubai-to-be-turned-into-nature-reserves-1.1615625689842)

### Inferences
- Generation rules:
  - (a) An organic inlet ~100–500 m wide at the NE end, crossing the corridor diagonally inland. Its extension continues as a ~100 m engineered canal lined by towers (Business Bay), then turns seaward as an 80–120 m canal through the villa belt to the beach, bridged where it crosses the spine.
  - (b) A ~3 km enclosed marina basin parallel to the coast at the SW super-node, ringed by towers, with a beach-front tower wall (JBR) on its seaward edge.
  - (c) Palm Jumeirah sits offshore between the two super-nodes. Its trunk is anchored roughly opposite the Media City/Al Sufouh area, approx., just NE of Marina. It is a ~5.6 km trunk-and-frond island of low villas on the fronds, with hotels on the crescent.
  - (d) A second, larger palm at Jebel Ali further SW.

### Gaps
- No total length for Dubai's natural coastline.

## 6. Dubai 2040 Urban Master Plan structure

### Takeaway
The plan organises growth around five urban centres: three existing ones in the coastal corridor (Deira/Bur Dubai, Downtown/Business Bay, Marina/JBR) and two new inland ones (Expo City, Dubai Silicon Oasis). Green corridors link them. Other headline targets are about 60% of the emirate as nature/rural land, 55% of residents within 800 m of mass transit, a 20-minute city, and a population rising from 3.3 M to 5.8 M.

### Cited Findings
- Five urban centres, three existing and two new: Deira & Bur Dubai, Downtown & Business Bay, and Dubai Marina & JBR, plus Expo 2020 Centre and Dubai Silicon Oasis Centre. — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/government/dubai-plan-2040-5-areas-to-see-massive-development); [Dubai Media Office](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2021/march/13-03/mohammed-bin-rashid); [Protocol Dubai, Phase II](https://www.protocol.dubai.ae/en/media-listing/news-events/mohammed-bin-rashid-approves-phase-ii-of-dubai-2040-urban-master-plan-reviews-progress-of-first-phase/)
- Roles: Marina/JBR is the tourism and lifestyle hub and DSO the technology and education hub (secondary source). — [Bayut](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/dubai-2040-master-plan/)
- Land use:
  - Hotel and tourism land rises 134%.
  - Commercial land rises to 168 km².
  - Education and health land rises 25%.
  - Green and leisure areas double (another source says +105%).
  - Nature reserves and rural natural areas make up 60% of the emirate.
  — [u.ae](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/strategies-initiatives-and-awards/strategies-plans-and-visions/transport-and-infrastructure/dubai-2040-urban-master-plan); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/government/dubai-2040-urban-plan-60-of-dubai-to-be-turned-into-nature-reserves-1.1615625689842)
- Green corridors will link service areas, residential areas and workplaces for pedestrians, cyclists and sustainable mobility. They will also connect major parks and natural assets. — [u.ae](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/strategies-initiatives-and-awards/strategies-plans-and-visions/transport-and-infrastructure/dubai-2040-urban-master-plan)
- 55% of residents should live within 800 m of main public transport stations, and density should increase around mass-transit stations. — [Bayut](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/dubai-2040-master-plan/); [u.ae](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/strategies-initiatives-and-awards/strategies-plans-and-visions/transport-and-infrastructure/dubai-2040-urban-master-plan)
- 20-minute city: 80% of residents within 20 minutes of daily needs on foot or by bike (secondary sources; some say "15-minute"). — [RTPI](https://www.rtpi.org.uk/new-from-the-rtpi/callum-campbell-mrtpi-dubai-2040-master-plan-creates-benchmark-for-urban-travel/); [Driven Properties](https://www.drivenproperties.com/blog/dubai-2040-urban-master-plan)
- Population grows from 3.3 M (2020) to 5.8 M (2040). — [Bayut](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/dubai-2040-master-plan/)
- More than 115 km of cycling and pedestrian tracks come under the Quality of Life Strategy 2033, which is separate from the 2040 plan. No "150 km green corridor" figure was found. — [bnw.ae](https://bnw.ae/en/blog/dubai-2040-urban-master-plan)

### Inferences
- Generation rule: the three coastal centres become the three main tower nodes along the spine (NE old core, middle Downtown, SW Marina). Two new mid-density centres sit inland, roughly beyond E311/E611: Expo City in the far SW interior near Al Maktoum airport, and DSO in the eastern interior on the Al Ain road side (approx.). Green corridors run as linear parks linking centres and estates, and density is concentrated within 800 m of metro stations.

### Gaps
- The official land-use table (percentage residential, commercial, industrial, etc. of the urban area) could not be retrieved because the dm.gov.ae executive summary PDF was blocked.
- No figures were found for the number, length or width of green corridors.

## 7. Height profile across the city

### Takeaway
Height peaks sharply at discrete nodes on the spine: Burj Khalifa (828 m) in Downtown, then Marina/JBR/JLT and the SZR wall, each with many 200–400 m towers. Height falls abruptly to 1–2 storeys in the villa belts on both sides. Between nodes, mid-rise (4–20 storeys) fills Al Barsha, Al Quoz edges, Tecom and JVC. The old core is low (1–3 storeys at heritage sites, mid-rise around them), and the outer suburbs are uniformly low.

### Cited Findings
- Six clusters together hold 309 buildings ≥100 m; Downtown+BB has 88, Marina 87, JLT 61 and SZR/DIFC 55. — [CTBUH](https://global.ctbuh.org/resources/papers/3816-Safarik_PolycentricCities.pdf)
- There are 73 buildings ≥200 m. Emirates Towers on the SZR strip is 333 m. Marina 101 is about 425–427 m. — [Wikipedia list via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Dubai); [Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/points-of-interest/emirates-towers/1322222); [emiratesbreaking](https://emiratesbreaking.com/area-guide/dubai-marina-towers/)
- Low-rise residential lies between SZR and the coast, getting denser toward the centre. — [Planetizen](https://www.planetizen.com/node/41919)

### Inferences
- Suggested height-field model: H(x,y) = max over nodes of peak_i · exp(−d_i²/2σ_i²), modulated by a band mask (coast-side villa bands capped at ~2 storeys) and a spine-proximity factor that decays over ~0.5–1 km from SZR. Approx. node parameters:
  - Downtown: peak 828 m (single supertall) with a 200–400 m ring, σ ≈ 1 km.
  - Business Bay: 150–300 m, σ ≈ 1 km.
  - SZR wall: 200–350 m, elongated ~3 km × 0.3 km.
  - Marina/JBR: 250–425 m, σ ≈ 1 km.
  - JLT: 150–350 m.
  - Deira: 80–150 m.
- Transitions are steep. Expect a tower-to-villa drop within one or two blocks (≈200–500 m) at JLT/Emirates Hills and Downtown/Jumeirah edges, rather than a smooth gradient.
- Burj Khalifa, Burj Al Arab and Marina should be recognisable landmarks. A single supertall should dominate the central node.

### Gaps
- No quantitative height-distribution data (e.g. median storeys by district) was found for non-tower districts. Typical storey counts for Al Barsha, JVC and Deira are not sourced.
